As we sat together and opened *Toxic Parents*, a book filled with warmth and strength, it felt as though we were also opening the carefully guarded chambers of our own hearts. We spoke about the tangled emotions that bind us to our families of origin, about the imbalance between obedience and genuine filial care, and about how the maxim “Parents are never wrong” had kept us from looking directly at our wounds. Through such honest sharing, we realised that the confusion and pain hidden within us had never been ours to bear alone.

We saw ourselves in Sandy: wounded by a parent’s words, yet diminishing the harm by insisting, “They only want what is best for me.” We also felt deeply for Les, forced into the role of a little adult in childhood and left, in later life, unsure how to love or be loved. Such toxic family patterns can become an invisible net, holding our feet fast as we move through life burdened by an abiding sense of unworthiness and insecurity. Yet the book speaks with clarity: we cannot choose the family into which we are born, but the direction of our life remains firmly in our own hands.

We can learn to become our own healers. We need not rush to forgive. First, we may allow long-suppressed anger and grief to find expression, and return responsibilities that were never ours to those who should bear them. We can establish a clear sense of self in our dealings with our parents—respecting their feelings without surrendering our own needs. We can stop punishing ourselves and understand that childhood pain was never a chain we deserved to carry. And when help is needed, we can courageously reach out, whether for a friend’s warm embrace or a professional’s support. No one should have to shoulder everything alone.

When the sharing ended and we gently closed the book, each of us carried a firmer conviction: our family of origin belongs to our past, but it is not the whole of who we are. We can move forward with our history without remaining bound by its wounds. We can remember an imperfect past while still daring to create a future filled with love and warmth. The right to choose remains ours. Through practice and every resource available to us, we can regain authorship of our lives. We cannot choose our parents, but we can choose our path: looking inward to discover our true selves, outward towards the wider world, and calmly seeking support when we need it. We will remain here, walking beside you. In this process, strength slowly gathers.

There is a song I love, and I wrote this entire reflection to its melody. It sings of all that the world gives us: the hum of insects and the crash of thunder, a crescent moon and evening stars; illness followed by gradual recovery, emptiness slowly filled by genuine feeling.

Our families may once have wounded us like thunder. They may have cast us into a valley like an illness, or answered our longing for love and recognition with emptiness. Yet through that very journey, we gradually learn to hear our truest needs. In repeated attempts to heal, we rediscover inner peace and strength. New experiences and new affections begin, little by little, to fill the hollow places within us.

Three passages from the book remained with us:

- Psychotherapy resembles emotional surgery. As with physical surgery, a wound must first be cleaned before it can heal, and pain may take a long time to subside. Yet when the pain begins, the work of healing has begun as well.

- Becoming a true adult is neither easy nor effortless. It asks us to endure frustration and mistakes and to temper ourselves completely. Anxiety, fear, guilt and confusion are unavoidable, but they no longer need to rule us. That is what matters.

- Love does not torment us, rob us of control, or teach us self-hatred. Love nourishes healthy feeling. To be loved is to feel accepted, cared for, cherished and respected; genuine love brings warmth, joy, security, steadiness and peace. Understanding love may force us to accept the sorrowful truth that our parents were unable, or did not know how, to love us. Once we see both their limitations and our losses clearly, however, a new door opens: we can welcome those who truly love us and receive the love we have always deserved.

So even if our family of origin once felt like a prison, let us believe this: the wounds that kept us awake will one day become nourishment for growth. The thorns we struggled to cut away will teach us to love ourselves with greater tenderness and resolve.

May you truly experience love in every relationship, with both the courage to receive it and the capacity to give it. And after every storm, may each path of healing lead at last to its own insectsong, crescent moon and evening stars—and to the genuine affection that gently fills the empty places of the heart.
