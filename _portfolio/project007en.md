Do you habitually tell the world that everything is fine, only to turn away and swallow your exhaustion alone, burying every doubt and grievance deep within? We are constantly told to be strong and independent. Far fewer people tell us that revealing our vulnerability and actively seeking companionship are not signs of weakness, but acts of courage through which we learn to treat ourselves with care.

<div style="text-align: center;">
  <img src="assets/img/portfolio/07-content-02.jpg" alt="" style="max-width: 80%; height: auto;">
</div>

Since this community began, most of our gatherings have been book clubs or relaxed, open conversations. That easy atmosphere has much to recommend it, yet something was always missing: confident speakers naturally occupied most of the time, while introverted, socially anxious or less articulate members rarely found an opportunity to share what was in their hearts.

With the wholehearted help of Long Shaonian and Xiaolu, this gathering became our first true peer support circle—a safe place to breathe for anyone caught in difficult emotions. There was no lecturing and no judgement, only acceptance and attentive listening. We established a few simple rules. The facilitator introduced a prompt, such as a small experience from the past month that had left us feeling low, then passed around a soft toy as the token for speaking. Only the person holding it had the floor. Receiving the toy never obliged anyone to speak; anyone who preferred silence could simply pass it on.

Round after round, this simple gesture restored balance to the conversation. No one was hurried or pressured, and everyone had an equal freedom to choose. Those who wished to speak could unburden themselves fully; those accustomed to silence were not overlooked. Each person was given an unhurried moment in which to be heard.

Life was never meant to be a solitary road endured through sheer force. We all need somewhere we can safely reveal our vulnerability and rest for a while. This new format compelled no one to share; its purpose was simply to create a sense of safety. Speaking can ease much of our pain, while listening can awaken profound recognition. When we discover ourselves in another person's experience, we realise that we were never the only ones struggling. The simple, sincere empathy and companionship between people can be the gentlest force for quieting the turmoil within.

Our first support circle brought many moments of warmth and moved us deeply. Several friends left feeling lighter and more at peace. In future gatherings, we will continue to protect this atmosphere of acceptance and ease. May everyone who joins us be able to set down the armour of forced strength, welcome every emotion, and gradually make peace with themselves in one another's company.
