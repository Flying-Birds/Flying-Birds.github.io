People sometimes ask, with genuine concern, whether spending a long time with those who are struggling emotionally might exhaust me and gradually diminish my own wellbeing. At first, such well-meant advice made me hesitate. After this gathering, however, I feel more certain than ever: creating this support group was the wisest and warmest decision I made all year.

We gathered to welcome a friend who had long been active in our online group but lived on the other side of the ocean. At the time, I was suffering from a stubborn cold and felt depleted in both body and spirit; even speaking required effort. I owe special thanks to Ninety, who took complete charge of the arrangements and made this in-person reunion a happy success.

  <img src="assets/img/portfolio/06-content-02.jpg" alt="" style="max-width: 80%; height: auto;">

Our gathering opened with a grand exchange of gifts. What touched me most was that Lin, returning from across mountains and seas, had specially brought me throat lozenges and cough syrup. Lao Kan had also been thinking of my health and thoughtfully prepared lozenges. These modest gifts overflowed with quiet tenderness: the care that remembers someone and reveals itself in the smallest details.

I recalled something Xiaolu had written in the group: whether this community began as an act of self-rescue or a wish to help others, its existence is meaningful in itself, because everyone within it is gentle and kind.

When I founded the group, I truly intended to help other people. I wanted to create a soft place to land for friends caught in low spirits and cycles of self-reproach, and to accompany them slowly out of life’s valleys. Yet over time I came to realise that, within this community, the person most often cared for, accepted and cherished was me.

A few days earlier, I had allowed myself a spell of sadness in the group chat. I had always felt conflicted: as founder and organiser, surely I should remain emotionally steady, restrain my darker feelings, and serve as everyone else’s support. But when I could no longer hold myself together, everyone met me with unconditional acceptance. They listened patiently, comforted me and helped me find perspective. In that moment, I felt the profound security of being received exactly as I was.

Many friends also wrote to me privately. They recommended trusted doctors and books, told me to call whenever I felt low, offered to share a drink whenever I needed company, and reminded me that I did not have to contain my sadness—I was free to cry.

These small, luminous moments brought tears to my eyes. I finally understood that there truly are people willing to make room for our emotions. We do not have to force ourselves to be strong at all times, and vulnerability is never mere self-indulgence. We need not perform the role of the composed, mature adult every hour of every day. We are ordinary people who also need to be understood, protected and emotionally held.

The first step towards genuine maturity is to face our emotions honestly, recognise our vulnerability, and accept and soothe the self who is passing through a low season.

Nor do healing and warmth move in only one direction. On the evening of the event, a friend came to confide in me. Having just been replenished by everyone’s kindness, I still carried enough warmth to hold his disappointment in turn.

That experience strengthened my belief that kindness, warmth and love always move in a circle.

It is also one reason I named our support group “Flying Birds.” Rabindranath Tagore’s classic collection *Stray Birds* takes its title from birds in flight—creatures that wander. I hope that everyone who has drifted from place to place, inwardly displaced and without a sense of belonging, may pause here and find a safe, warm home for the spirit. Here, no one should need to pretend, suppress themselves, or maintain a persona. A moment of emotional collapse, an aimless stream of everyday thoughts, or the smallest details of an ordinary life may all be expressed freely and without fear.

May this become a gentle island of safety, where ordinary people can lean on one another and heal in both directions; where companionship eases loneliness and tenderness quiets the turmoil within; where, in all the days ahead, we steady one another and continue forward together.
