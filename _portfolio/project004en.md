In a support group, people naturally come and go. One day, Lao Kan asked why someone had suddenly left the group chat. In the past, the question would have sent me into immediate panic, checking over and over to discover who had gone. I have always been unusually sensitive to parting, anxious that the people around me might disappear without warning.

For years, that anxiety lived within me like a fine thorn: a profound fear of abandonment. Whenever separation arrived unexpectedly, I never first considered that there might be another explanation. I turned the accusation upon myself. Had I spoken too harshly the last time we talked? Was I simply not good enough, and was that why they no longer wanted me in their life? An endless spiral of self-reproach followed.

The death of a relative could leave me grieving for years. After a breakup, I remained trapped in my feelings, unable either to accept the ending or to let go. Even when parting from a friend, I lacked the courage to say a proper goodbye.

Now, after passing through one loss after another, I see that I had been trying to hold relationships in place through anxiety and people-pleasing. I had forgotten that genuine connection is never secured by binding someone with the fear of losing them.

Little by little, I began moving from anxious attachment toward a more secure way of relating. I cannot claim that all anxiety has vanished; memories of those who left can still taste bittersweet. Yet I no longer resent the people or experiences that once unsettled me. Instead, I feel a deep gratitude. Bereavement taught me to cherish those who remain. A relationship’s end taught me that love requires mutual acceptance. The departure of friends taught me that “goodbye” may also carry the promise, “until we meet again.”

More importantly, this growth taught me to live in the present. I no longer cling to the question, “Why must they leave?” I cherish the people still beside me—the friends who share their hearts with me and laugh with me about life—and I have become better able to receive the small, warm moments hidden in our time together.

This morning, Ninety shared two new art prints in the group: Van Gogh’s *Sunflowers* and *The Starry Night*. I smiled at the screen. By coincidence, *Sunflowers* hangs in my living room, while a magnet I bought at the National Gallery in London last year bears *The Starry Night*. I hurried into the living room, photographed the painting and magnet, and sent the picture to Ninety. She replied, “Of all the things in the world, somehow we share these two.” Across the screen, I could almost see her eyes light up. The unexpected affinity was like a piece of sugar, filling the heart with warmth.

That afternoon, while we were singing together, Xiao Jiang began choosing songs. She was born after 2010, so I assumed I would recognise none of her choices. To my surprise, every song she selected was a classic I loved. We sang together, from Teresa Teng to Jay Chou, and the distance between our ages disappeared into the music. What truly brings people closer is never age or occupation, but kindred spirits revealed through what they love. Even with more than a decade between us, one beloved song can bring two people into harmony within the same melody.

These small moments shone like stars, gradually illuminating a truth within me. On the train of life, some passengers arrive earlier and others leave sooner; no one accompanies another all the way to the end. Yet the people who remain beside us for each stretch of the journey are not there by accident. They have crossed mountains and seas to reach us. They stay not because we are perfect, but because somewhere—in an experience, a feeling, a painting, a song, or a shared love of life—we happen to resonate.

The “cherishing” I have now learned means receiving these precious affinities with both hands. I am grateful to those who once walked beside me: they brought their stories into my life and helped me shed my immaturity and grow. I also want to care well for those who still choose to remain, nurturing the rare warmth of being on the same wavelength.

May we treat every meeting as a gift from heaven, and treasure every season of companionship deep within the heart.
